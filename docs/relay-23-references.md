# The vehicle Nika and Oleg found

The starting point is the first Kharkovchanka built in Kharkiv in 1958. Its driver, mechanic and sleeping crew occupied one insulated body. The later Kharkovchanka-2 moved the cab forward into a separate unit. For Relay 23, the former expedition complement has dwindled to two people on opposite watches. The open aisle, radio bench, grow rack and trade with Six are part of their refit.

## Surfaces, fittings and reuse

- Falcon’s enamelware starts with formed steel and a fired glass coating. A contrasting rolled rim and dark steel exposed locally at a contact edge give the galley kettle two materials a visitor can recognize. [Falcon manufacturing notes](https://us.falconenamelware.com/pages/faq)
- Bendigo Military Museum’s canvas haversack has two 25 mm brass flap buckles, a widening shoulder strap and rear attachments. Use that scale for the chart satchel. Auckland Museum’s tin documents another practical joint, a wire handle pivoting on thin pressed metal. [Haversack](https://victoriancollections.net.au/items/6179dd6fa912eb19596fb95a) · [Mess tin](https://www.aucklandmuseum.com/collection/object/930204)
- Two reusable candidates were scoped as alternatives. Guy in a Poncho’s *Camping Cooking Pots* offers four objects at about 13k triangles and four 2K material sets under CC BY. GenPBR’s CC0 canvas supplies a tileable weave. The refit uses original geometry and surface work sized for these bays. Neither candidate ships. [Sketchfab listing](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/camping-cooking-pots-3cf2b0e0d285408f97e2a33d6f19ee1c) · [Canvas Fabric](https://genpbr.com/materials/canvas-fabric)
- Snupp’s painted exterior remains the shipped CC BY 4.0 asset. The repository contains its optimized GLB. The second exterior source archive could not be located locally for a matched comparison.
