# terminal-portfolio

My portfolio as a working terminal: the same ASCII art and look as my [GitHub profile](https://github.com/ChristenJacquottet), terminal colour schemes, and every published case study from [christenjacquottet.com](https://christenjacquottet.com) as a file you can `ls`, `cat` and `open`. Type `exit` to step back from the screen: it is an Apple II on the radio bench of a rebuilt 1958 Kharkivchanka, and you can keep typing on it.

```console
$ ls -l ~/shipped
$ cat ~/shipped/world-cup-host
$ grep voice
$ projects | grep award
$ theme amber
$ exit
```

## Run it

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173
npm run build      # static site in dist/
```

## What it does

- **A real shell, small.** Folders and files, `cd`, `ls -lt`, `cat`, `tree`, `open`, pipes into `grep` and `head`, `;` and `&&`, tab completion for commands, paths and themes, and history on ↑ ↓ that survives a reload. `help` lists the rest; a few commands aren't listed.
- **Clickable.** Every name in the output is a button that types and runs its command, so nobody has to know Unix to read it.
- **Colour schemes.** `theme` lists them with their ANSI palettes: `github` (white and green, like the profile README), `phosphor`, `amber`, `homebrew`, `pro`, `solarized`, `dracula` and `dos`. `theme <name>` switches, and the choice is saved, shown in `~/.cjshrc` (`ls -a`). `crt off` drops the scanlines and glow; reduced-motion settings start with it off.
- **The desk.** `exit` loads a three.js scene: the radio compartment of relief crawler No. 23 at polar dusk. Click a porthole for night watch, or the shortwave receiver for the crew’s last dispatch. [Setting and model references](docs/relay-23.md). The terminal stays DOM (through CSS3D, so text stays sharp and typing still works) and sits *under* the WebGL canvas, which draws the curved glass as a hole with darkened edges and a reflection of the window, so the flat page reads as a curved CRT. Stepping back is one move: the screen narrows into the shape of the glass at the same text size, keeps its scrollback, and the camera carries the motion on, zooming at an even rate. Outside the portholes is an Antarctic relay hut across the ice. The ice lights the bulkhead alongside the desk lamp from its bulb (with a glow inside the shade), a warm room fill in the home-lab corner, and the screen, which lights the keys in the theme's colour. The camera looks down at the desk nearly straight on, the computer on a cutting mat in the middle and a big world map behind it. Shadows are real but redrawn only when something that casts one moves (a light changing, the camera turning or a clock hand ticking doesn't redraw them), and a light that's switched off doesn't redraw its own. The terminal boots first (~41 KB gzipped); once it goes idle on desktop or tablet broadband (never on phones, `Save-Data` or slow links), the desk chunk (~207 KB gzipped) and ~3.9 MB of 3D assets (the computer 733 KB, the props 2.9 MB, and 248 KB of room textures) prefetch and pre-parse quietly at low priority so the first `exit` is usually already at 100%, with shaders compiled and textures uploaded behind the fullscreen terminal before the flight starts. Phones stay terminal-first (zero 3D download unless you run `exit`, which loads a lighter mobile GPU profile). It only renders while something moves, never faster than 60 frames a second (120 Hz screens would otherwise draw twice as often), and uses a fixed pixel ratio up to 1.5 on desktop so the picture stays steady during a drag. You sit at the desk: drag to turn your head (with a mouse or a finger; the head stays put), scroll or pinch to lean in or back. Navigation props bring their output up on the physical monitor. Click the Apple II to frame it, click again to enter the terminal, or press Escape to return to the desk.
- **Touch anything.** Hovering a prop names it, with a soft rim of light; clicking does what it would do. Things move the way they would: hung things swing from their nail, a toss lands with a small second bounce, the coin rocks flat on its edge, the lamp's filament catches with a flicker, and the window slides the room from dusk to night over a second instead of cutting. Knock the mug and the coffee sloshes against the cup and rings, then settles, with steam while it's warm (`src/mug.ts`). The floppy box holds a disk for each featured project: pick one and it goes into the Disk II and opens the case study. The phone gets you in touch, the Walkman opens, seats either Awesome Mix on its two spindles, and plays the album through Spotify, the lamp switches off (and the room goes to moonlight), the globe spins, the camera flashes, the Rubik's cube turns its faces (and solves itself if you leave it), the bitcoin flips. Everything can also be done from the terminal.
- **The lab.** Past the end of the desk, the rack: the PVM's status tube, the switch with its link lights (the activity ones flicker on a one-frame-a-second heartbeat while the desk is in view, and never when a frame is slow), the NAS, the C64, two laptops in a vertical dock on a build server, and, made in code (`src/lab.ts`), a Raspberry Pi cluster, a UPS, the ethernet runs from the switch's lit ports, a soldering station, a power strip mounted under the network shelf with its service loops, a parts organiser, a spool of cat6, spare drives, and a roll of tape, a hank of cable and a steel rule on the pegboard.
- **Sound.** Key clicks, switches, the disk drive and the tape, mostly synthesised in the browser (`src/audio.ts`), in a small synthesised room (a short convolution that falls away inside the screen), each cue panned to where its prop is on screen, the keys with the case's knock under them, a quiet, directional rack fan and wind and occasional grains of ice against the hull; a few CC0 and public-domain recordings in `public/audio/` (sources in `public/audio/CREDITS.md`). The wind and the tube's hum come up as you lean back from the screen and go quiet when you sit down. `sound off` or the button at the desk mutes it; `/?soundtest` auditions every cue (22 of them).
- **The things on the desk** are true to me: a wooden racquet and a pennant (college tennis), a globe and a print of Réunion's peaks (where I was born), a Super 8 camera and a 35mm camera (filmmaking and photography), a graph-paper notebook (computer science), and a "CAN YOU HEAR ME?" card by the phone (the video call where the mic died and we held up paper). The rest comes from my old "what's on your fridge" timeline: a cork board of every place home has been (Réunion, French Guiana, Normandy, Indiana, London, Dallas), a steer skull (DFW), a rugby ball, a skateboard (I built them for friends), my first bitcoin, the baccalauréat and a grad cap, a clapperboard (directing and shooting), a toy plane and a suitcase, and a pixel portrait from collecting digital art.
- **Arrange it yourself.** Open `/?edit` (on `npm run dev`) and the desk comes up with a layout editor: click any prop, or pick it from the list, then move (G), rotate (R) or scale (S) it with the gizmo, or type exact numbers; H hides it, F flies the camera to it, shift snaps. The camera is free while you edit. Changes are kept as a draft in the browser; **save to layout.json** writes them into `src/layout.json`, which is what visitors get, and **copy json** puts the same thing on the clipboard. The editor is its own chunk and never loads outside edit mode.
- **Shareable.** `/#cat ~/shipped/super-bowl-ai` opens the terminal and runs the command.

## Deploy

Vercel builds it as is (`vercel.json`): `npm ci`, `npm run build`, serve `dist/`. It's a static
site, so any static host works the same way. Hashed bundles in `/assets` are cached forever;
the models, textures and audio for an hour, then revalidated in the background, so updated
gear reaches visitors within the hour. There's no CI: check locally with
`npm run typecheck && npm run build && npm run preview`.

## Content

`src/content/generated.json` is generated; don't edit it by hand.

```bash
npm run sync   # needs ../personal-portfolio (with node_modules) and ../ChristenJacquottet
```

The sync loads projects through the portfolio's own publication resolver, so the terminal can only show what the site publishes. Copy comes from the site's `content/site.ts`, and the art from the profile repo's `ascii-art.svg`. Hand-written bits (folder names, short names, open-source list) are in `src/content.ts`.

## The model

`public/models/apple-ii.glb` is built from the Sketchfab download with `npm run model -- path/to/scene.gltf` (114 MB → ~733 KB via `scripts/optimize-model.mjs`). The bench and fitting packs in `public/models/` contain the Poly Haven and Sketchfab props listed in `scripts/build-props.sh`, each restyled towards one palette in Blender first (`scripts/restyle.py`, `art/restyle.json`, `art/palette.json`) and compacted with per-prop screen-size WebP texture budgets and attribute-aware meshopt simplification that keeps edges within 0.15% of their size (`scripts/optimize-props.mjs`), built with `npm run props -- path/to/downloads` (Blender, or the `bpy` wheel on Python 3.13 via `BPY_PYTHON`; the props near the camera build from their 2K sources and `scripts/upgrade-props.mjs` swaps one prop's textures in place). The oak textures are encoded by `scripts/room-textures.mjs`, with half-size copies for phones. Sources, budgets and better candidates: `docs/assets-sourcing.md`. The oak pedestal desk (with hardcoded extents), the wheelhouse, radio cabin, berths, galley, grow rack and service bay, the racquet, ball, notebook, card, pennant, print, mug, floppy box, bitcoin, Rubik's cube, grad cap, cork board, diploma and pixel portrait are made in code (`src/room.ts`, `src/cabin.ts`, `src/quarters.ts`, `src/props.ts`).
